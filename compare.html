M

Monarch Money

vs BOCY

Monarch is a well-designed personal finance dashboard — for Americans. It connects to US bank accounts via Plaid, categorises transactions, and gives you a clean view of your net worth and spending.

But that's where it stops. Monarch tells you where your money is. It doesn't tell you where it should be. There's no rate comparison, no savings optimisation, no actionable insights based on your surplus. It's a rear-view mirror, not a GPS.

And it doesn't work with UK banks at all.

Monarch watches your money. BOCY puts it to work.
C

Cleo AI

vs BOCY

Cleo is a chatbot that roasts you about your spending habits. It's fun, Gen-Z-flavoured, and decent at giving you a nudge when you overspend. The auto-save feature can help you build a buffer.

But Cleo is a spending cop, not an earning optimiser. It doesn't know what interest rate your savings account pays. It can't tell you that moving £5,000 from a 1.5% account to a 4.39% account earns you an extra £144/year. It was built primarily for the US market, with limited UK support.

Cleo judges your spending. BOCY grows your savings.
AI

ChatGPT & LLMs

vs BOCY

You can ask ChatGPT "where should I put my savings?" and get a reasonable-sounding answer. The problem? It will confidently tell you a savings account pays 4.50% when it actually pays 2.05%. LLMs don't have access to live rate data — they hallucinate numbers.

They also don't know your financial situation. They can't see your income, your bills, your existing accounts. Every answer is generic. And they can't act — they can't move money, set up transfers, or monitor rate changes over time.

BOCY uses AI where it helps (analysis, explanation, insights) but grounds everything in verified, real-time data from your actual accounts.

LLMs guess at rates. BOCY knows them — and acts on them.
